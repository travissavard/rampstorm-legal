1. Who we are and what this policy covers

Rampstorm is an arcade flight game about flying through storms, made by ProductSeal ("we", "us"), a sole proprietorship based in the United States. This policy covers the Rampstorm app for Android and this website. It explains what data is involved when you play, who handles it, and the choices you have.

For the personal data we receive ourselves (the emails you send us and the order records Google Play gives us as the seller), ProductSeal is the "controller" under data protection law. Google and Unity decide for themselves how they use the data their software collects in the game, so each of them is a separate controller of that data. We have not appointed a data protection officer. You can reach us at dev@productseal.com about anything in this policy.

2. What we collect ourselves

ProductSeal does not run a server for Rampstorm, and the game contains no analytics or crash-reporting service of ours. The game's own code does not send your gameplay, progress, settings or identity to us or to anyone else. That means we do not collect or receive:

  • your name, email address, phone number or any account (there is no sign-in);
  • your location (the game does not ask for location permission);
  • your contacts, photos, files, microphone or camera (never requested);
  • your advertising ID, your IP address or other device identifiers;
  • your game progress, scores, settings or play time.

We do receive personal data in two situations:

  • If you email us. We then have your email address, what you write and anything you choose to include, such as your Support ID, a screenshot or a Google Play order number. We use it only to reply and to deal with your request.
  • If you buy something. As the seller, we receive Google Play's order and sales records, described in section 7. They do not include your payment card details.

5. Advertising: Google AdMob, Unity Ads and consent

Rampstorm shows one kind of ad: a rewarded ad that you choose to start, usually a short video and sometimes an interactive ad, in exchange for bonus Coins. There are no ads when you open the game, between flights or during a flight. Watching ads is optional and never required to play or to finish the game.

Ads come from Google AdMob through the Google Mobile Ads SDK, made by Google LLC. Consent is handled by Google's User Messaging Platform (UMP). AdMob uses mediation: it may fill an ad request from Unity Ads (Unity Technologies), whose software is also built into the app for that purpose. When the game checks your consent status, loads an ad or shows one, Google, and Unity when AdMob uses it, may collect the data below. Google's and Unity's software may store and read information on your device, such as identifiers, to do this. The data goes to them, not to us, and each uses it under its own privacy policy as a separate controller.

This data is only about ads. Nothing about how you play is collected, because Rampstorm has no gameplay analytics. The ads software encrypts what it sends over the network. The game does not give the ads software your Support ID or any other identifier of ours.

Consent where the law requires it. In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, Google's consent message appears before any ad is loaded, and the message lists the companies your choice applies to. If you agree, ads may be personalised. If you do not agree, Google may still show limited or non-personalised ads that use less data, as described in Google's policies, or no ads. Your answer never affects free play.

Extra protection in Europe. Where Google's consent tool reports that EU-style data protection law applies to you, the game also marks every ad request as coming from someone under the age of digital consent, because some countries set that age as high as 16 and the game does not ask your age. Google then turns off personalised advertising for those requests, whatever you choose in the consent message.

What kind of ads. The game limits ads to content Google rates as suitable for general audiences (rating G).

Read how Google and Unity use this data:

  • Google Privacy Policy: https://policies.google.com/privacy
  • How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services: https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites
  • Unity Game Player and App User Privacy Policy: https://unity.com/legal/game-player-and-app-user-privacy-policy

We do not receive your advertising ID, your IP address or your location from Google or Unity. We only see totals, such as how many ads were shown and how much they earned, in our AdMob account.

6. Your ad choices and how to change them

  • In the game. Open Settings, go to Account & privacy and tap Privacy choices. The same button is on the Sponsor Kiosk, where rewarded ads are offered. Where Google's consent tool offers choices, this opens Google's form so you can see, change or withdraw your consent at any time. If you are in a US state with a consumer privacy law, this is also where you can opt out of the sale or sharing of personal information and targeted advertising. If no choices apply where you are, the game tells you so.
  • On your device. You can reset or delete your advertising ID in your Android settings. On most devices it is under Settings > Privacy > Ads or Settings > Google > Ads, depending on your Android version. Deleting it stops ads personalised with that ID across your device.
  • With Google. You can manage ad personalisation for your Google Account at https://myadcenter.google.com/.
  • Simply do not watch ads. Every ad in Rampstorm is optional, so no ad plays unless you start one. The consent check when the game starts, and the loading of ads in the background described in section 3, still involve your IP address and device information. To avoid them completely, play offline.

7. Purchases: Google Play Billing

Everything you can buy with real money in Rampstorm, such as coin packs, Hangar Kits, theme packs and direct unlocks, is sold through Google Play using the Google Play Billing Library. Google handles the payment. We never see or receive your card or bank details, your billing address or your name.

  • What the game receives from Google Play: the products and prices on sale, which products your Google account owns, whether each purchase is completed or pending, and the purchase details Google signs so the game can check they are genuine. The game uses this only to give you what you bought.
  • What the game keeps on your device: for each purchase, the product, the date, the price and currency Google Play showed, its status, a short support code shown in Account > Purchase history, and a one-way fingerprint of Google Play's purchase token (not the token itself). It is part of your profile and never sent to us.
  • What the game gives Google Play: when you start a purchase, the game passes Google Play a one-way scrambled form of your Support ID. Google can use it to help detect fraud, and it becomes part of the order record Google keeps and makes available to us as the seller. It does not reveal your identity, and the game does not use it to track you.
  • What we receive as the seller: Google Play gives us order and sales records for Rampstorm purchases, such as the order number, date, product, price and taxes, the buyer's approximate location (for example country, state, city or postal code) and the device model. They do not include your name, email address or payment details. We use them for accounting and tax, to handle refunds and purchase problems, and to prevent fraud. We share them only where needed with tax authorities and professional advisers such as accountants. We do not run a server that checks your purchases.
  • Google keeps your order history in your Google account under the Google Privacy Policy.
  • Refunds are handled under Google Play's refund policy. If a purchase is refunded, Google Play stops reporting it, and the game may remove what it unlocked the next time it checks (see the terms of use).

8. Ghost Races (switched off in this version)

Rampstorm contains code for Ghost Races, a planned online feature for racing other pilots' recorded flights through the same storm. In version 1.0 it is switched off. The Multiplayer tile shows "Coming soon", and the game makes no Ghost Races network request, so nothing is uploaded to or downloaded from a ghost server.

While Ghost Races are off, the game still keeps your best run on each Attack course on your device (your control inputs, your plane's build and your time), so that you could choose to share it once the feature exists. It stays on your device and is not sent anywhere.

Before Ghost Races are ever switched on, we will update this policy with exactly what they collect, who can see it, how long it is kept and how to delete it, and we will update the game's Google Play data safety information to match.

10. Children

Rampstorm is made for players aged 13 and over. Its target audience on Google Play is 13 and over, and it is not directed at children under 13. The game does not ask anyone's age, has no chat and gives players no way to send anything to other players.

Ads in the game are limited to content Google rates as suitable for general audiences. Wherever Google's consent tool reports that EU-style data protection law applies, such as in the European Economic Area and the United Kingdom, the game marks ad requests as coming from someone under the age of digital consent, so ads there are not personalised (section 5).

We do not knowingly collect personal data from children under 13. If you are a parent or guardian and believe your child has sent us personal data, for example in an email, contact us at dev@productseal.com and we will delete it. To remove game data from the device, use Delete profile or clear the app's storage as described in section 11. To reset data used for ads, reset or delete the device's advertising ID as described in section 6.

13. Your privacy rights

Depending on where you live, privacy laws give you rights over your personal data. In plain terms:

Europe and the UK (GDPR and UK GDPR)

  • Access and portability: ask what data is held about you and get a copy. For data on your device, Account > Export my data copies your profile to the clipboard; your settings are shown on the Settings screen.
  • Correction and deletion: have wrong data corrected, or data deleted.
  • Restriction and objection: limit how your data is used, or object to uses based on legitimate interests.
  • Withdraw consent at any time through Settings > Account & privacy > Privacy choices.
  • Complain to a data protection authority, for example where you live or work. The EU authorities are listed at https://www.edpb.europa.eu/about-edpb/about-edpb/members_en. In the UK it is the Information Commissioner's Office: https://ico.org.uk/make-a-complaint/.

United States: California (CCPA and CPRA) and other states

Residents of California and of other states with consumer privacy laws (such as Colorado, Connecticut, Virginia, Utah, Texas and Oregon) may have the right to:

  • Know and access the personal information held about them, and get a copy in a portable form;
  • Correct or delete it;
  • Opt out of the sale or sharing of personal information and of targeted advertising, using Privacy choices in the game or by deleting your advertising ID (section 6);
  • Appeal if we decline a request, by replying to our answer with the word "Appeal". We answer appeals within 45 days. If you disagree with the outcome, you can contact your state attorney general;
  • use an authorised agent to make a request for you, where state law allows. We may ask the agent for proof that you authorised them;
  • be free from discrimination: you will not be treated differently for using these rights.

14. International transfers

Google and Unity are based in the United States and process ad and payment data in the United States and other countries. They protect transfers of personal data out of the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland with legal safeguards such as the European Commission's standard contractual clauses and, where they take part in it, the EU-US Data Privacy Framework, as described in their privacy policies.

If you email us, your message is stored by our email provider and read by us, which may be in a country other than yours. Where the law requires it, we rely on an adequacy decision or standard contractual clauses for that transfer.

15. Security

Game data is kept in the app's private storage and protected by Android's app sandbox. The game keeps only a one-way fingerprint of Google Play's purchase tokens, not the tokens themselves. Google's and Unity's software encrypts data it sends over the network. We do not store your game data on any server, which removes a whole category of risk. No system is perfectly secure, so keep your device updated and protected with a screen lock.
